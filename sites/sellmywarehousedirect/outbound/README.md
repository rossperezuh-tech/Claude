# Outbound kit: going to owners directly

The site and ads catch owners who are already searching. This kit is for reaching owners who
are not searching yet, which is where most of the volume is. Start with Houston.

Everything in brackets, like `[First Name]`, is a blank to fill in.

## 1. Pull the list

Source: the Harris County Appraisal District (HCAD) public property data. Other counties have
the same data from their own appraisal district.

Filter to:

- **Property type:** industrial and warehouse buildings. In Texas appraisal data these fall
  under state class F1 (commercial real property) and F2 (industrial real property); narrow F1
  down to warehouse building types. Check the code list in the file you download, since each
  district labels them a little differently.
- **Size:** building of roughly 10,000 sq ft or more, or whatever matches your $500,000 minimum.

Then rank owners by how likely they are to sell. Each of these is a point:

- Owned 15 years or more
- Owner's mailing address is out of state, or different from the property address
- Owned by a person, a family trust or a small LLC rather than a fund or REIT
- Property taxes are delinquent
- The building looks vacant (no business at the address, no recent permits, boarded or overgrown)
- Open code violations
- Owner name includes "Estate of" or the owner is deceased

Start with the 500 to 1,500 owners with the most points. A smaller list contacted five times
beats a big list contacted once.

For LLC owners, look up the registered agent and managers through the Texas Comptroller or
Secretary of State records, then skip trace the person.

## 2. Track what works

Put a different web address on each mail drop so the lead form records where the lead came
from. Use this pattern:

    sellmywarehousedirect.com/sell-warehouse-houston-tx?utm_source=mail&utm_campaign=hou-letter1

Change the last part for each piece (`hou-letter2`, `hou-postcard1`). A QR code that points to
that address works well on postcards. Phone calls will not carry this, so ask every caller,
"How did you hear about us?" and write it down.

## 3. Letter sequence

Send plain letters in a plain envelope with a real stamp. Three weeks apart.

### Letter 1: the introduction

> [First Name],
>
> I'm writing about your property at **[Property Address]**.
>
> My company buys warehouses and industrial buildings in the Houston area, and I'd like to
> make you a cash offer on yours.
>
> We are the buyer, not a broker. That means no listing, no commission and no repairs. We buy
> as-is and can close in as little as 14 days, or later if that suits you better.
>
> If you've thought about selling, even a year or two from now, I'd like to talk. Call or text
> me directly at **361-537-7255**, or send the details at **[tracking web address]**.
>
> If you're not interested, no problem, and thank you for reading.
>
> [Your Name]
> Sell Your Warehouse Direct
> Houston, TX

### Letter 2: the follow-up

> [First Name],
>
> I wrote a few weeks ago about **[Property Address]** and wanted to follow up once.

> a short phone call. You don't need to clean it out, fix anything or move a tenant.
>

> money, or they are simply ready to stop being a landlord. If any of that is close to your

### Letter 3: the last one

> [First Name],
>
> This is my last letter about **[Property Address]**. I don't want to keep filling your

### Postcard (between letters 2 and 3)

Front: **WE WANT TO BUY [PROPERTY ADDRESS]**

Back:

> Cash offer in 24 hours. Close in 14 days or on your schedule.

> We are the buyer, not a broker.
> Call or text 361-537-7255 · [QR code to tracking address]

## 4. Phone script

Call after letter 1 has landed. The goal of the first call is a conversation and permission to
follow up. You are not trying to agree on a price.

**Opening**

> "Hi, is this [First Name]? This is [Your Name]. I sent you a letter about the building at
> [Property Address]. Did I catch you at an okay time?"

**If yes**

> "I'll be quick. I buy industrial buildings in the area, and I'm calling to see whether you'd
> ever consider an offer on that one."

**Questions to ask, then listen**

1. "How long have you owned it?"
2. "Is it occupied right now, or sitting empty?"
3. "What kind of shape is the roof and the building in?"
4. "Is there a loan on it?"
5. "If you did sell, what would you want to do with the money or the time?"
6. "Is there a number you'd need to see to consider it?"

**Close of the call**

> "Thanks, that helps. Let me look at the property and come back to you with a written offer
> by [day]. What's the best way to send it to you?"

**Voicemail**

> "[First Name], this is [Your Name] at 361-537-7255. I sent you a letter about your building
> at [Property Address]. I'd like to make you a cash offer on it. No broker, no commission.
> Call or text me at 361-537-7255. Thanks."

**Common replies**

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "It's not for sale." | "Understood. If that changes, would it be all right if I checked in once or twice a year?" |
| "What's your offer?" | "I want it to be a real number, so I need a few details first. Can I ask you four quick questions?" |
| "I'd want [high price]." | "Thanks for telling me. Let me run it and I'll show you how I get to my number, and we can see how far apart we are." |
| "I have a broker." | "No problem, I'm happy to work through them. Who should I call?" |
| "How did you get my number?" | "From public property records. I'm reaching out to owners of industrial buildings in the area. I can take you off my list if you'd prefer." |
| "Take me off your list." | "I will, right now. Sorry to bother you." Then do it. |

## 5. Rules to follow

- **Do-not-call:** check numbers against the National Do Not Call Registry before cold calling,
  and keep your own list of people who ask not to be contacted.
- **Texts:** do not send marketing texts to people who have not given you their number and
  permission. Text only people who contacted you first or agreed to it.
- **Autodialers and recorded messages:** do not use them for cold calls to mobile phones.
- **Be accurate:** you are a principal buyer. Do not imply you are the owner's agent or that
  you have a buyer waiting.

These rules carry real penalties. Have your attorney confirm your process before you scale it.

## 6. Follow-up schedule for every lead

Most deals come from follow-up. Put every lead on this schedule until they sell or say stop.

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Within 5 minutes of a web lead | Call. If no answer, leave a voicemail and send an email. |
| Same day | Second call attempt at a different time of day. |
| Day 2 | Call, then email the written offer or a request for the missing details. |
| Day 5 | Call. |
| Day 10 | Email: "Still interested in an offer on [Address]?" |
| Day 21 | Call. |
| Then monthly for 6 months | Call or email with something useful: a recent sale nearby, a change in your offer. |
| Then quarterly | A short check-in. |

Log each lead with: date, source, property address, owner, what they said, their number, your
number, and the next follow-up date. The next follow-up date is the field that matters most.
