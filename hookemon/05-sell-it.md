# Sell It

Principle: **make a sound within 5 seconds, talk second.** Customers decide with their ears and hands, not the spec sheet.

## The 30-second demo structure

1. **Hook (0–5s):** something is already playing, or you hit play immediately.
2. **One move (5–20s):** do *one* impressive thing live: flip an effect, sample their voice, mute and drop the drums.
3. **Hand it over (20–30s):** put it in their hands with one instruction ("hold this and turn that").
4. **Who it's for (one line):** "This is for people who want to ____."

Then stop talking and let them play.

## Common objections

Practice these in pairs. Answers are starting points; put them in your own words.

**"Isn't this a toy?"**
> Hand it over and make something with it. Then: "Artists use these on records and on stage. It's small so you'll actually take it with you."

**"Why does it cost so much?"**
> "You're paying for build quality and an instrument designed so you finish ideas instead of fiddling. It replaces a laptop, interface and controller for a lot of people." Have a cheaper option ready (Pocket Operators, EP-133).

**"OP-1 field or OP-XY?"**
> "OP-1 field if you want to sketch, record and sample, tape-style. OP-XY if you want deep sequencing and to perform with it live." Ask how they make music now.

**"Does it work with my computer / DAW?"**
> Yes, over USB (audio + MIDI on most units — verify per product). Know which ones need a cable or adapter.

**"I don't know how to make music."**
> Start them on a Pocket Operator or EP-133. Get them to a beat in under a minute.

**"Can I use it with my other gear?"**
> Sync, MIDI, audio in/out. Know the answer per device. This is where support-trained staff win.

## Floor habits

- Keep each demo unit loaded with a project that sounds good the second you press play.
- Reset demo units at open and close.
- Know one song or artist that uses each product.
